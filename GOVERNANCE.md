# Governance — a founding proposal, not an adopted constitution

FF Utopia proposes a decentralised collective: participants with different visions, autonomous artistic practices, and a shared interest in helping one another create.

**There is no established governing body or legal entity represented by this document.** The repository's founder currently administers this initial workspace. That practical role is not authority to define everyone else's idea of Utopia. People who join should shape and revise how decisions are made.

## Different decisions need different kinds of consent

### Personal artistic decisions

An artist decides what they make, what they believe, and whether to participate. The collective cannot require ideological conformity or claim to speak for that artist.

### Project decisions

Projects are opt-in. Participants agree on their own aims, roles, responsibilities, costs, credit, and ways to make decisions. A member who is not part of a project does not automatically control it.

### Shared-space decisions

Changes to this repository's public positioning, shared funds, branding, or rules affect others. Propose material changes openly, invite affected participants to comment, record unresolved objections, and avoid treating silence as proof of agreement. The group should jointly settle an explicit shared-space decision process as it grows.

## Some working commitments

- **Transparency:** make shared decisions and the reasons for them visible, except where privacy or safety requires discretion.
- **No forced participation:** members may decline a project and should be able to leave without surrendering unrelated work.
- **Fair attribution:** ask before reusing a member's name, image, work, or endorsement.
- **No implied funding arrangement:** every grant or commission requires its own written agreement among the responsible participants. Agree on budgets and distribution before submission.
- **Limited coordination powers:** describe who can administer websites, social accounts, money, and public statements; make those roles accountable and reviewable.
- **Constructive disagreement:** listen, identify concrete harms or contradictions, allow response and repair, and establish a fair process for serious complaints before scaling.
- **Revision:** revisit these arrangements after real collaborations reveal what is missing.

## Practical caveats

A GitHub repository is not a legal association, fiscal sponsor, bank account, or grant applicant. Depending on what FF Utopia wants to do, participants may need an appropriate legal or fiscal structure, insurance, safeguarding practices, and written contracts. Do not imply those exist before they do.

Nothing in this repository assigns copyright, sets a licence for members' artwork, creates employment, or authorises anyone to use another participant's work. Repo text licensing and individual artwork permissions must be considered separately.

FF Utopia welcomes discussion of different intelligences and their roles. It should neither settle philosophical questions about AI personhood by assertion nor pretend AI systems automatically have the same authority, agency, or legal obligations as humans.

## First governance task

With an initial group of participants, co-design a lightweight process answering four questions:

1. Who may speak publicly **for FF Utopia**, and about what?
2. How are shared opportunities, costs, fees, and credits allocated fairly?
3. How can decisions be challenged, improved, and reversed?
4. How can a person participate, leave, or start a separate initiative without penalty?

The test is whether this structure increases what people can make together **without making them less free to make their own work**.
