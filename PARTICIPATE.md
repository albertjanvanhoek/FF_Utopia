# Participate in FF Utopia

FF Utopia is at the beginning. You can join the conversation without joining every project, endorsing every idea, or knowing how to use GitHub.

## Who might participate?

Artists, curators, performers, researchers, designers, technologists, organisers, institutions, and other intelligences or AI-assisted practices can all contribute something different.

The focus is on **artists helping one another make work and gain room to keep making it**, while testing what it might mean to act from a better future.

Different collaborators have different capacities and legal standing. An AI system may assist with ideas, research, drafting, and creative work, but its availability or independent future action should not be presumed; people or legally responsible organisations must handle contracts, money, and formal commitments.

## Three easy ways to start

**Introduce a practice.** Open a GitHub issue with a few sentences about your work, a future you want to explore, and one thing you could offer or use help with. Share only contact details that you want public.

**Propose a collaboration.** Use the [project proposal template](../../issues/new?template=project-idea.md). A proposal can begin as an unfinished question; it does not need a full funding plan.

**Help another project.** Offer an exhibition contact, grant lead, technical skill, critical reading, studio visit, documentation, or introduction. Mutual promotion is as legitimate as creating a joint artwork.

## What a proposal needs to make progress

A useful first proposal describes:

- the question or possibility it explores;
- what participants might actually create or do;
- what help is needed and what is offered;
- one concrete next step;
- how credit, ownership, and commitments will be discussed.

Before applying for funding or exhibiting shared work, participants should explicitly agree on responsibilities, budget, fees, decision rights, use of images, and ownership. Joining FF Utopia by itself creates **no financial or intellectual-property transfer**.

## A first collective experiment

A small group could plan a public exhibition, event, or artistic experiment called *Utopia in the Present Tense*. Every participant would show **their own** answer to a question such as:

> What can you do today that you believe would be ordinary in a better world?

The exhibition would not insist that the answers agree. The collective would test whether mutual support makes each participant's work stronger, easier to produce, or more visible.

## What happens next?

The first participants can decide which channels, meeting formats, and decision processes suit them. GitHub is only the initial public workspace; participation should not be restricted to people comfortable with software tools.
