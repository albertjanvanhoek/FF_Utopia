# The FF Utopia Playbook

How a club without a captain actually plays. This is a living document — any member
can propose changes by pull request. **These are proposed ways to coordinate, not an
already-ratified constitution.** The real test is whether they help members make things.

---

## 1. Decision-making: no captain, still a team

**Not all decisions belong to the same circle.**

- **Your own play:** start it yourself. You do not need a club vote to make your own art, explore a question, or offer help. Just don't imply everyone endorses your position.
- **A joint project:** participation is opt-in. Its participants decide its scope, credits, costs, rights, and working arrangements together. Other members are neither automatically enlisted nor entitled to direct it.
- **A decision for the club:** common name, shared public statements, shared money, common infrastructure, and standing rules need an announced proposal and a visible record of the outcome. The people or organisations whose money, work, or legal responsibilities are involved must give **explicit agreement**.

**Consent, not forced consensus.** For a substantive club-wide proposal, put the text in an issue or pull request, notify accessible active members, and leave at least two weeks for reasoned objections. Try to improve the proposal in response. An objection should identify a concrete risk or harm, not simply a different artistic taste. Silence is not proof of endorsement. If an objection remains unresolved, try a reversible **opt-in pilot**, not a rule imposed on everyone. Before there are enough members to define a better process, the repository maintainer records which decisions have actually been agreed and which remain proposals.

**Rotating tasks, not permanent captains.** A grant-calendar editor, event host, or repo maintainer can serve for a season, share what they know, and hand over where feasible. GitHub permissions are technical roles, not an artistic hierarchy; only accounts with access can actually merge.

## 2. Grants: the shared pipeline

- **Grant calendar:** open calls and deadlines go in [`grants/calendar.md`](grants/calendar.md). Check an official source, eligibility, and the date before sharing an opportunity.
- **Review buddies:** any member can ask for a second pair of eyes on an application. Members help if and when they can.
- **Co-applications:** where a call fits several members, apply together *only after* agreeing on the eligible legal applicant, lead, work packages, budget, fees, ownership, and reporting responsibility. Until FF Utopia has an eligible legal structure, don't imply the club itself can receive a grant.
- **Recycled knowledge:** with the applicants' permission, share lessons and **redacted** examples in `grants/archive/`; never publish unapproved proposals, reviewers' comments, personal data, or financial identifiers.

The calendar is an index, not a guarantee that a call is open. See [grant workflow](grants/README.md).

## 3. Work: making together

- A work lives in `works/project-name/` with a short README. Use [the work template](works/_TEMPLATE.md) to state what the work is, who's opted in, what it needs, and how it plays forward Utopia.
- Be explicit about whether a project is an *idea*, *seeking collaborators*, *in progress*, or *completed*. An idea alone does not obligate anyone.
- **Credit and rights:** agree on authorship, permissions, image use, licences, compensation, costs, and withdrawal *before* submitting a joint grant or committing work for public display. A GitHub upload is not permission to exploit a member's artwork.
- **Keep the commons alive:** record what you learned, whose conditions for making work improved, and what you would change next time.

## 4. Matchdays: exhibitions and events

A **matchday** is any public moment of the club: an exhibition, concert, reading, screening,
open studio, lecture, or online release.

- Each confirmed matchday has a host (rotating) and lives in `matchdays/YYYY-MM-DD-name.md`.
- A proposed event stays labelled as a proposal until a host, venue/platform, date, basic resources, and participating artists have actually agreed.
- Members can attend, share, or contribute; nobody is obliged to. Ask before advertising another member as a participant.
- If it's practical and welcome, the match can end with the victory hum — *Seven Nation Army*. The riff's familiarity is part of the fun, but playing, recording, sampling, or distributing the song in public may require the relevant permissions.

One possible first matchday is [*Utopia in the Present Tense*](works/utopia-in-the-present-tense/README.md).

## 5. Promotion: a member's win is a club win

- **The pass rule:** when promoting your own work, look for chances to promote another member's work you genuinely appreciate. It's an invitation to reciprocity, not an enforced quota or automatic endorsement.
- New work, shows, releases, and awards go in [`news.md`](news.md) so everyone can pass the ball.
- Ask before using other members' images, testimonials, names as endorsements, or unpublished material. Respect refusals.

## 6. Money (when there is any)

- Any common resources should primarily enable member work and matchdays. This does not mean individual artists must work for free.
- A project must name who can legally sign, receive, spend, and account for funds **before** a financial commitment is made. No member or AI collaborator can commit someone else merely by speaking in the club's name.
- Be transparent about funding sources, budgets, allocation criteria, total receipts and spending, and potential conflicts of interest **without publishing** private bank details, personal financial records, contracts, or confidential funder material.
- If FF Utopia later needs a legal home, the group can examine options such as a Dutch *vereniging*, a foundation, or working through an existing fiscal host. That requires a specific decision, not an assumption.
- Resources and benefits should not quietly concentrate with the most vocal or best-connected members. Check who gets opportunities and who does the invisible maintenance work.

## 7. Playing against Apocalypse

We don't fight people. Team Apocalypse isn't a group of enemies — it's the drift towards
fear, extraction, isolation, and despair that anyone can slip into, including us.
We beat it by playing better, not by hating harder.

A club that becomes coercive, extractive, or incapable of learning has stopped playing its own game. Listen when a teammate points that out.

## 8. How we know whether a play helped

After a collaboration, ask:

1. **Created:** What work or public experience actually happened?
2. **Enabled:** Did any participant gain a useful contact, income, audience, skill, resource, or continuing opportunity? Who didn't?
3. **Cost:** Whose time, money, care, or reputation did the play consume? Was that agreed and fair?
4. **Learned:** Which expectations were wrong, and what would we do differently?
5. **Passed on:** What can another member reuse without having to start over?

There is no leaderboard, compulsory impact score, or single official picture of success. Be candid about failures. A small, honest pass can matter more than an impressive-sounding plan.
