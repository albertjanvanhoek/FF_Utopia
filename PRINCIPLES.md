# Principles to practice — draft

FF Utopia is meant to learn. These principles are a **starting proposal**, not a test of ideological purity or a finished constitution.

## 1. Start living parts of the future now

An artwork can show a possible world. The way we make, fund, exhibit, and share that artwork can also demonstrate it. We try to make the process consistent with what the work asks of the world.

## 2. Utopia is plural

People can care about different futures and disagree about important things. Membership does not mean adopting one comprehensive vision. We seek enough common ground to act, while leaving room for genuine difference.

## 3. Be autonomous and interdependent

Each participant keeps their own practice, voice, and agency. Collaboration should expand, rather than replace, what participants can do independently. Leaving a project should be possible without unnecessary penalty.

## 4. Help one another persist and develop

Artists need time, materials, audiences, livelihood, care, space, and criticism. We try to improve these enabling conditions for one another. Reciprocity need not mean identical contributions, but arrangements should be fair and made explicit.

## 5. Turn disagreement into learning

Welcome questions, unexpected perspectives, and correction. Distinguish observations from interpretations; acknowledge uncertainty and mistakes. We can identify specific problems without requiring agreement about everything else.

## 6. Share credit and resources fairly

Agree on responsibilities, recognition, ownership, fees, costs, and grant distribution before a collaboration makes binding commitments. Do not assume that sharing an idea transfers its intellectual property. Avoid extracting unpaid work under the banner of a good cause.

## 7. Care for what makes creation possible

A thriving artistic culture depends on human relationships, technical systems, institutions, and the living world. Treat their maintenance as creative work, not an afterthought.

## 8. Keep power visible, limited, and revisable

Coordination is necessary; unchecked authority is not. Roles should be understandable, accountable, and, where feasible, shared or rotated. No one can declare their preferred vision to be the official definition of Utopia for everyone.

## 9. Try things and show what happened

Prefer reversible experiments and tangible projects over abstract perfection. Share results and failures. If a practice harms its participants or fails to achieve what it promised, change it.

---

**An open question:** Which principles make cooperation possible across very different artistic and philosophical visions, and which are themselves open to revision? We should keep that question alive.
