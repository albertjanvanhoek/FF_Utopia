# The grant relay

A grant lead becomes useful when somebody can *act* on it. The club should help members find opportunities, make stronger applications, and learn from both success and rejection. It must not turn into a system in which the best-connected member absorbs all the benefit.

## 1. Find and verify a lead

Add it to the [grant calendar](calendar.md) with the **official** funder link, submission deadline (including timezone), and a “last checked” date. Check discipline, location, entity type, project stage, eligible costs, and whether individuals or groups may apply. If still uncertain, label it `To verify`.

A call is not live just because it appears in our table; check the funder's site before starting work.

## 2. Match people to the opportunity

Share the lead even when you are not eligible yourself. A member may volunteer to be a **lead**, an applicant, a participant, a reviewer, or simply a source of information.

Do not nominate someone or put their name on a submission without their agreement. A shared application must say who benefits and why the collaboration is worth the cost.

## 3. Agree before writing the joint proposal

Record, privately where necessary:

- The **eligible legal applicant** and who can sign the application, contracts, and reports.
- Each person's artistic scope, responsibilities, decision rights, and delivery commitments.
- Budget and cash flow, including fees for artistic and organisational labour, production, overhead, and contingencies.
- Authorship, rights to exhibit/reproduce work, reuse of material, and approval for publicity.
- Who bears a funding shortfall, and how to resolve a disagreement or participant withdrawal.

**A proposed club name is not a legal grant recipient.** Use an eligible applicant or fiscal partner unless a proper FF Utopia entity has actually been created.

## 4. Review and submit

Ask members for feedback or an application review, with enough time and information to be useful. Treat funder restrictions and unpublished ideas as confidential. The authorised applicant checks the funder's current terms and submits.

## 5. Learn and pass the knowledge on

After a result, share what can safely be shared: suitability of the call, how much effort the application took, what reviewers found unclear, whether the promised artistic benefits appeared. Archive **redacted, permission-cleared** lessons in `grants/archive/`.

Never commit passwords, identification documents, bank records, unpublished applications without approval, or personal contact information to this public repository.

## What counts as a good grant outcome?

A submission is not automatically a win. Ask whether it improved artists' ability to produce and sustain their work, and whether the time and resources spent were worthwhile. A rejection that produces a reusable insight can still be a useful pass.
