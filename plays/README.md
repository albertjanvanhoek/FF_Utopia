# Pass the ball: small plays

A club becomes real when members help one another **do** things. Not every useful collaboration needs a manifesto, a committee, or a grant.

A **pass** is a small, freely offered contribution that gives another member more room to create: a useful introduction, grant lead, application review, equipment loan, studio visit, constructive critique, audience, or technical help. A request for help is equally welcome.

## Make a pass

1. [Open a pass issue](../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/pass.md) or copy the [pass template](_TEMPLATE.md).
2. Say **what you can offer or need**, and any limits on your time, availability, or resources.
3. Let people opt in. Agree privately, if necessary, on contact details and anything confidential.
4. When something happens, report the *useful outcome*, if everyone involved is comfortable. For example: “A review helped us clarify our proposal,” not “We helped someone.”
5. Note what you learned. Sometimes the honest result is “this didn't work.”

A pass need not be public, reciprocated immediately, or recorded at all. We share examples to help others learn, **not to measure who owes whom**. Don't publish another person's contact details or private requests without permission.

## Two hypothetical first passes

- **Grant relay:** one artist spots a relevant open call, another checks eligibility, a third reviews a draft. Each keeps their right to say no. If they apply together, they agree on a responsible applicant, ownership, fees, and reporting first.
- **Audience relay:** two members make very different work. They visit each other's studios, ask difficult questions, and share what genuinely interests them with their own audiences. They don't have to endorse one another's worldview.

## A more ambitious play

A group can turn a pass into a [work](../works/_TEMPLATE.md), [grant proposal](../grants/README.md), or [matchday](../works/utopia-in-the-present-tense/README.md).

**A pass is successful when another participant gains a real option they did not have before, without losing agency they wanted to keep.** Sometimes the gain is simply a better understanding of why an idea won't work.
