# Proposed first matchday: Utopia in the Present Tense

**Status: Idea — not yet a scheduled or funded exhibition.**
**Seeking:** artists, a possible host/venue, curatorial partners, and people interested in helping shape the experiment.

This proposed matchday is one possible episode within [FF Utopia as a living artwork](../../EXPERIMENT.md). The club's relationships and working practices are part of the larger experiment; the exhibition can make them visible without claiming to be its conclusion.

## The question

> What could you do or make today that you believe would be normal in a better world?

FF Utopia starts with a peculiar claim: we can pull a fragment of a possible future into the present by **practising it now**. An exhibition could make that claim visible, test it, and allow visitors to disagree with it.

## Format to explore

Invite a small set of artists to propose **different**, even conflicting, visions. Each presents a work, performance, gesture, workshop, or public experiment. The works do not have to illustrate a shared ideology.

One space might explore abundance, another care, another power, another ecology, another what it means to learn with machines. Those are examples, not prescribed themes. The curatorial challenge is to make the differences **productive**, without forcing a tidy consensus or pretending real contradictions disappear.

The event's production could itself be a work: can a group share space, attention, production knowledge, and credit in ways they think a better art world would?

## A tiny first version

1. **Invite a few artists** to respond to the question and say what kind of presentation would suit their work.
2. **Find a willing host or space** and choose a realistic format: one evening, a weekend, or an online release. Nothing is booked yet.
3. **Create an opt-in production plan:** roles, venue access, care/accessibility needs, budget, artist fees, technical demands, publicity, ownership and image permissions.
4. **Make a public moment** in which the art gets to be art, not a compulsory sermon. Invite audience questions and disagreement.
5. **Reflect together:** what really became possible through the collaboration? Which costs were unseen? Which Utopian behaviours survived contact with reality?

## What would make it successful?

Not just attendance. Look for useful new relationships, fairly paid or transparently agreed work, new artistic possibilities, audience engagement, and insights worth passing to the next project. Note when collective process *reduced* someone's autonomy or added unpaid work; that's a result worth learning from too.

## First actual step

Find interested artists and a willing coordinating host. Until those people opt in, this is **a proposal by an AI collaborator**, not an event announced on behalf of the club.
